Label: Polyvinyl Records

General Comments / Reviews:

Dreamy, drone-flecked, breakbeat-driven indie electronica from L.A., with critics reaching for Seefeel, Broadcast and Stereolab. Two FCC tracks late in the sequence (9 and 10). - DJ Stace  [DRAFT, edit or replace]

“a softly-lit and pastel-hued otherland easy to get lost in” - The Line of Best Fit

“sonically disorienting but also incredibly cohesive” - Clash

Release Notes:

Recent album from Los Angeles band Dummy, their first for Polyvinyl. Earlier records: debut Mandatory Enjoyment (2021), Free Energy (2024) and the remix album Bubbelibrium DLC (2025). Built from the band's own recorded sample library, chopped up and reassembled, with guitars pushed back (Northern Transmissions, Treble).
Emma Maatman - keyboards, vocals; Nathan O'Dell - guitar, vocals; Joe Trainor - guitar (also a publicist); Alex Ewell - drums, vocals; Lance Umble - bass. Guests: Nina Pitchkites ("Song of Mana"); Allyson Foster and Paul Benson of New Issue ("Alluvial Fan").
Produced, recorded and mixed by Dummy and Joo-Joo Ashworth, in a home studio and at Studio 22, Los Angeles. Ashworth also made Dummy's Mandatory Enjoyment there (Discogs) and has produced Froth and Holy Wave records. Mastered by Mikey Young of Eddy Current Suppression Ring and Total Control.
The band says the songs take on climate degradation, A.I. and the devaluation of art, and the surveillance state (Polyvinyl). Label comparisons: Seefeel, Disco Inferno, early Bjork, Madonna's Ray of Light.

Tracklist:
1. April Limousine 05:03 - Uptempo (~145 BPM). Stuttering psychedelic breakbeat that melts into a slow, ethereal float, with little recognizable guitar (Treble, Northern Transmissions). Minimal and repetitive, Emma Maatman's vocals used like another instrument, building to a climactic coda (Clash, Treble).
2. Alluvial Fan 03:39 - Midtempo (~100 BPM). Funky, slacker-ish mood piece (Stereogum). Precise guitar and drum rhythm, shared vocals from Maatman, Ewell and O'Dell, plus guest vocals from Allyson Foster and Paul Benson of New Issue (AudioFuzz).
3. Full Spectrum Dominance 03:35 - Midtempo (~108 BPM). Wobbling wah-wah guitar and a lumbering, baggy bassline (Northern Transmissions, Treble), like Stereolab remixed by Lemon Jelly (Line of Best Fit). Pulls back to a heartbeat pulse, breaks down into distorted guitar, then settles to a steady groove that leads into the next track (Clash).
4. Squeaky Wheel 06:23 - Mid to uptempo (~128 BPM). Long, repetitive and textural, spiralling upward around layered Maatman vocals (Northern Transmissions, Treble). Hectic but inviting (Glide).
5. Touch the Perfect Speed 04:29 - Mid to uptempo (~120 BPM). Keeps a strong rhythmic emphasis inside hazier textures (AudioFuzz).
6. Song of Mana 06:22 - Uptempo (~130 BPM). Starts as a wistful ambient-pop drift, then hardens into acid-techno squelch (Northern Transmissions, Treble). Gently angular synths and cryptic vocals, a strange video-game-soundtrack feel (Line of Best Fit). Guest vocals: Nina Pitchkites.
7. Omen Globe 02:35 - Uptempo (~141 BPM). Short, strobing, dizzy rush of ravey shoegaze (Treble, Northern Transmissions). Punk-tinted (Glide).
8. C.B.B.S.I.M.M.P.W.M. 01:38 - Uptempo (~142 BPM). Instrumental (wordless on Genius; Clash says the band lean into the instrumental side). Short, works as a breather between louder tracks (AudioFuzz).
9. N of 1 05:03 - FCC “fucking” x1 (Verse 2). Midtempo (~96 BPM). Locks onto a single groove with huge drums and smeared guitar drones, glacial guitar cut into a jungle-inspired drum setting (Line of Best Fit, AudioFuzz). Sticky, dancefloor-ready feel (Line of Best Fit).
10. Heaven's What You Make of It 04:27 - FCC “piss” x1 (Verse 2). Pace: check (BPM source says ~164 BPM, but critics call it restrained). Driven by an infectious bassline with strong Maatman vocals (Treble). Opens and closes like a portal, with reversed synths and rippling beats (Line of Best Fit). Closer.
