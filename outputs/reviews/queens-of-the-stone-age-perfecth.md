Album / Artist: Perfecth / Queens of the Stone Age

Label: Matador Records

Release Date: October 30, 2026
Review Date: 10/18/2026
Reviewer: DJ Stace

General Comments / Reviews:

Out Oct. 30. Moody, melodic, love-obsessed QOTSA with strings and acoustic guitars as often as riffs, produced by Homme and Shuman. Easy Street is FCC and five tracks are still unscreened. - DJ Stace  [DRAFT, edit or replace]

“a direct and emotionally exposed album exploring love, heartbreak and recovery” - Record Collector

“minimalist riffs with an explosive chorus” - NME (on Where The Goth Girls At?)

Release Notes:

Josh Homme - vocals, guitar; Troy Van Leeuwen - guitar; Michael Shuman - bass; Dean Fertita - guitar, keyboards; Jon Theodore - drums. Guest: Nikki Lane on Easy Street (Homme produced her 2022 album Denim & Diamonds). Wikipedia also lists Dave Grohl as an additional musician with no track named; unconfirmed.
Produced by Josh Homme and Michael Shuman. Homme co-wrote and produced Iggy Pop's Post Pop Depression (2016); Shuman also plays in Mini Mansions. Tracked from New Orleans to Los Angeles (Matador); Wikipedia lists Sound City in Los Angeles.
Mark Rankin engineered and mixed, with Alan Moulder and Finn Howells also mixing. Rankin recorded ...Like Clockwork and mixed In Times New Roman... for the band. Mastered by Dale Becker (Wikipedia).
Ninth QOTSA album, after In Times New Roman... (2023), on Matador. Homme's other bands: Kyuss, Eagles of Death Metal, Them Crooked Vultures.
Matador says the nine songs map different kinds of love: family, friends and others. Homme says he broke the word "perfect" to make the title.

Tracklist:
1. It's Only Love 03:07 - FCC unverified: no lyrics posted. Jangling guitars and strings, closer to Wilco than desert rock (Electric Eye). Strings curl around wordless vocal sighs (Record Collector). Train sounds at the open and close (Electric Eye).
2. Insignificant Other 05:53 - Loose, mellow, late-night groove: electric piano, a bass groove and stumbling guitar accents under crooned vocals (Revolver, Music Talkers). Homme says it is about distance.
3. Where The Goth Girls At? 03:28 - Staccato stomp: minimalist riffs, explosive chorus, string and horn arrangements (NME, Revolver). Falsetto vocal (Revolver), bass-led (Clash). Homme says it draws on his teenage years in Palm Desert.
4. Mono 03:19 - FCC unverified: no lyrics posted. Mix moves from mono to stereo, from claustrophobic to widescreen orchestral (Matador). Spectral melody over an almost trip-hop rhythm (Electric Eye). Drifting feel (Record Collector).
5. From the Cliff 02:58 - FCC unverified: no lyrics posted. Acoustic ballad (Electric Eye).
6. Sunday 04:55 - Re-recording of the Post Pop Depression song (Iggy Pop, 2016; written by Josh Homme and Iggy Pop, Wikipedia). Most danceable track here (Electric Eye). FCC screen is from posted lyrics for an unreleased recording, so confirm by ear.
7. Easy Street 03:51 - FCC “fuck” x1 (Verse 2). Features Nikki Lane, duet vocals. Gentle, acoustic-led (Rock Sound, Earvolt): acoustic guitar, hand percussion, woozy synths, an eBow guitar bliss-out at the end (press reviews). Almost operatic folk strain (Electric Eye). Made like a demo, no click track (Homme).
8. Phantom Limb 04:55 - FCC unverified: no lyrics posted. Grungy and folk-inflected (Electric Eye).
9. Run Away From the Mob 03:58 - FCC unverified: no lyrics posted. Starts slowly, recalls Pavement, gains weight in the last quarter (Electric Eye). Album closer.
