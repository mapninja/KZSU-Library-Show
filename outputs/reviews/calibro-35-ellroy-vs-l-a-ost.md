Release Notes:

Enrico Gabrielli - piano, keyboards, sax, flute, bass clarinet; Massimo Martellotta - electric guitar, sitar guitar, synthesizer, organ; Fabio Rondanini - drums, percussion; Roberto Dragonetti - bass guitar. All instrumental.
Produced and mixed by Tommaso Colliva, who co-founded Calibro 35 and is also long-time engineer for Muse (Sound On Sound) and producer of Afterhours. Recorded by Colliva and Simone Bertorello at Laboratori Testone (Milan); mastered by Giovanni Versari at La Maestà (Milan).
Milan band, together since 2007, that began by re-recording Italian cop-movie (poliziottesco) themes. Their funk has been sampled by Dr. Dre (One Shot One Kill) and Jay-Z. Earlier scores include Sandokan and Blanca; this is their eighth soundtrack (Rockit).
Score for Francesco Zippel's documentary Ellroy vs L.A., a portrait of author James Ellroy and Los Angeles that shows the band recording. Martellotta says they scored Ellroy's interview as if it were a spoken-word piece (Il Giornale della Musica).
Several titles are Ellroy novels (The Black Dahlia, The Big Nowhere, L.A. Confidential, White Jazz). Sound is crime funk and cinematic jazz with psychedelia, blaxploitation touches and Morricone echoes (Rockon, OndaRock).

Tracklist:
1. Black Dahlia 03:17 - Instrumental. About a minute of organ and building sound, then the full band kicks in with sharp, syncopated hits (Rockit). Cinematic crime funk.
2. LSD on Sunset Blvd 03:36 - Instrumental. Gabrielli's winds (sax, flute) out front (Il Giornale della Musica).
3. Pacific Safari 03:46 - Instrumental. Hypnotic psychedelia over a crime-jazz base (OndaRock).
4. The Big Nowhere part 1 04:01 - Instrumental. First of two parts. Smoky, watery, nocturnal jazz with improvised-sounding horns up front; the rhythm comes in only near the end of the pair (Rockit, OndaRock).
5. L.A. Confidential 04:35 - Instrumental. Longest track. Morricone-style phrasing with a mystery-film melancholy (Il Giornale della Musica); crime funk with cinematic tension, psychedelia and a hypnotic groove (Bandcamp).
6. Riots 02:37 - Instrumental. Boom-bap-like drum groove, twangy electric guitars answering an insistent clavinet riff; tension builds and resolves only at the end (Rockon). Nervous electric funk, close to the band's early cop-movie work (Il Giornale della Musica, OndaRock). Tempo sources disagree (~90 or ~180 BPM; the boom-bap description points to ~90).
7. Chalet Manson 04:03 - Instrumental. Minimalist, widescreen cinema feel (Il Giornale della Musica). Contains a nod to Franco Battiato's "Shock in My Town" (Rockit).
8. The Big Nowhere part 2 02:41 - Instrumental. Second of two parts. Same smoky, nocturnal jazz feel (Rockit, OndaRock).
9. Vacation and Probation 02:34 - Instrumental. Hot, aggressive funk (OndaRock) with acidic, dissonant synths recalling 1970s and 80s Italian crime films and tastefully distorted guitars (Cromosomi). Band calls it one of the most cinematic tracks on the score.
10. White Jazz 02:55 - Instrumental. Opens on brushed drums, then a swaying, slowed-down, nocturnal groove (Rockit).
11. Feels Like Home 04:08 - Instrumental. No description found.
12. Moonlight Sonate 02:18 - Instrumental. Closer, shortest track. The band's own respectful take on Beethoven's Moonlight Sonata (Ludwig van Beethoven, 1801) (Il Giornale della Musica).
