Album / Artist: Inflatable Graveyard Vol. 2 / Tropical Fuck Storm

Label: Fire Records

Release Date: October 16, 2026
Review Date: 10/18/2026
Reviewer: DJ Stace

General Comments / Reviews:

Live art-punk, noise-rock and psych-blues from Melbourne, caught over three nights in New York with The Saints thrown in for good measure. Say TFS on air (the band's own shorthand; the full name is an FCC word), and tracks 1, 2 and 7 are FCC too. - DJ Stace  [DRAFT, edit or replace]

Release Notes:

Second live album from the Melbourne band, recorded over three nights of a sold-out residency at TV Eye in New York. All eight tracks are live versions of earlier songs, plus a cover. Follows the first live album, Inflatable Graveyard.
Lineup: Gareth Liddiard - guitar, vocals; Fiona Kitschin - bass, vocals; Erica Dunn - guitar, keyboards, vocals; Lauren Hammel - drums.
Lineage: Liddiard and Kitschin come from the Melbourne rock band The Drones, and TFS formed when the two took a break from it. Dunn also plays in Mod Con, Harmony and Palm Springs; Hammel drums in High Tension.
Song sources: Fairyland Codex (2025) for tracks 1, 2, 3, 4 and 6; Braindrops (2019) for track 5; the debut A Laughing Death in Meatspace (2018) for track 7. Track 8 is a cover of The Saints, the 1970s Brisbane punk band. The studio albums are credited as written and produced by the band.

FCCs: 1,2,7

RIYL: The Drones, King Gizzard & The Lizard Wizard, The Birthday Party, Viagra Boys, Black Midi

Play: All but FCCs, Favs Rated with up to *****

Tracklist:
1. Irukandji Syndrome 05:28 - FCC “shit” x1 (Verse 4, studio lyrics). Studio version: snide talk-sung lead over bright three-part harmonies from Kitschin and Dunn, kinetic bass and busy drums (Fairyland Codex reviews). Live, it opens into a long guitar solo in the manner of The Velvet Underground's "I Heard Her Call My Name" (Fire Records).
2. Bloodsport 03:36 - FCC “cunts” x1 (late in song, about line 35 of 48; Genius posts no sections; studio lyrics). Shortest track. Groove-fueled with punchy, stuttering guitar breaks and a funk-inflected Talking Heads feel; one critic hears surf rock. Kitschin and Dunn vocals are featured (Fairyland Codex reviews).
3. Goon Show 05:52 - Caution “assholes” x1 (early in song, about line 6 of 40; Genius posts no sections; studio lyrics). Studio ~99 BPM (Shazam). Slow, foreboding march with a demented guitar solo, percussive and syncopated lead vocal, chorus harmonies that snap (The Needle Drop).
4. Stepping on a Rake 04:39 - Ballad: gently plucked guitar over sparse drums, tender and folk-like in the studio version, with heavier undertones live (Fairyland Codex reviews; Fire Records). Dire, sparse guitar backdrop.
5. Who’s My Eugene? 06:05 - Studio ~84 BPM (Shazam, Chordify). Restrained, funky groove, called a Martian funk jam, with a cooing female lead vocal (Treble, DIY; sources differ on whether Dunn or Kitschin sings).
6. Moscovium 07:14 - Longest track. Slow build in a heavy, sinking minor key to a noisy peak with shouted vocals and guttural, warzone-like guitar (Cyclic Defrost, studio version). Sounds more urgent live (Fire Records).
7. You Let My Tyres Down 05:20 - FCC “shit” x1 (Verse 2, studio lyrics). Studio ~133 BPM (SongBPM). Huge, fuzzed-out, noisy guitar riffs with a loping, shambling feel, a boozy early Nick Cave vibe (reviews of the studio version).
8. This Perfect Day 03:37 - Cover of The Saints' "(This) Perfect Day" (written by Chris Bailey and Ed Kuepper, 1977). The Saints' original is raw, fast 1970s Australian punk with buzz-saw guitars.
