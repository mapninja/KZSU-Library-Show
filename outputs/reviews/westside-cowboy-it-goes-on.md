Release notes (research, edit freely):
- From Manchester, UK.
- Produced by Loren Humphrey.
- Debut full-length from the Manchester four-piece: Reuben Haycocks (vocals, guitar), Jimmy Bradbury (vocals, guitar), Aoife Anson-O'Connell (vocals, bass) and Paddy Murphy (drums, vocals). All four share writing credits, and three of them take turns singing lead (Haycocks sings 7 tracks, Bradbury 3, Anson-O'Connell 1).
- 11 songs, about 35 minutes. Out on Island Records (Universal) on vinyl, CD and digital.
- Produced, engineered and mixed by Loren Humphrey (who produced their 2026 EP So Much Country 'Till We Get There and has worked with Geese and Cameron Winter). Recorded mostly at Greenmount Studios in Leeds, mixed at Magic Mountain in Tuxedo Park, NY, and mastered by Felix Davis at Metropolis in London.
- The last two tracks come from pre-production sessions in Humphrey's living room in upstate New York. The band says it wanted the record to sound young, immediate and a little rough.
- The band calls its sound 'Britainicana', meaning British indie rock crossed with American folk and alt-country. They formed in 2023, three of them met at the Royal Northern College of Music, and they won Glastonbury's Emerging Talent Competition in 2025.
- The songs keep coming back to time moving and things in motion. The band picked the title after noticing that, though Haycocks says it is not a concept album.
- Critics loved it: Metacritic 87 (universal acclaim), Stereogum Album of the Week, Pitchfork 7.8, Paste A, NME 4/5, DIY and musicOMH 4.5/5, Rough Trade Album of the Month for September 2026. It reached #5 on the UK Albums Chart.
- Singles: 'Kick Stones (The Boys)' (May 20, 2026, released with the album announcement), 'Pin Up Boys' (July 1, 2026) and 'Dobro' (August 6, 2026).
- Apple Music (US) marks only track 8, 'Coyote', as Explicit. A second 'It Goes On' listing on Apple Music is probably a clean edit (unverified).

From Pitchfork (Victoria Borlando):
“tightly wound indie rock and sharp, thoughtful songwriting. It's a winning combination.”

Tracks:
1. ____ “Kick Stones (The Boys)” 3:56 - [your notes] [Lead single (May 20, 2026). Its video was made with F.C. United of Manchester. The band cut its big live arrangement back to a slow build, using a live Velvet Underground 'What Goes On' as the model, and Haycocks calls it the album's sonic touchstone. Pitchfork hears the VU fuzz sped up and sharpened. NME says its momentum will make you smile.]
2. ____ “Paper Chains” 2:51 - [your notes] [Listed as 'Paperchains' on Apple Music and Genius. Haycocks almost dropped it. Pitchfork notes Vampire Weekend-style guitar trills in the breakdown. The Soundboard points to the chaotic drumming at the end, and The Skinny found parts dreary before the arrangement opens up.]
3. ____ “Dobro” 2:45 - [your notes] [Third single (August 6, 2026). Bradbury wrote it quickly on a four-track cassette recorder after a tour. Murphy calls it the band's first 'strutting' song, with a relaxed groove.]
4. ____ “Well Done Kid, You Did It” 3:27 - [your notes] [Haycocks wrote it while forcing himself to finish a song a day. Written under the influence of Modest Mouse, and he describes it as an 'emo dance track'. One of the simpler lyrics on the record.]
5. ____ “Worried Age” 3:58 - CLEAN (one non-profane 'God' in verse 1) - [your notes] [Pitchfork calls it the album's centerpiece. It started as a 10-minute jam inspired by LCD Soundsystem and New Order before being cut down, and Future Islands' In Evening Air shaped the final version. It is about what it feels like to be their age right now.]
6. ____ “Big Wheels” 3:17 - [your notes] [A Bradbury song from the band's early days, about repetition and feeling stuck. NME points out the Blockbuster DVD imagery. Beats Per Minute called it a misstep that stalls the album's momentum.]
7. ____ “Pin Up Boys” 2:45 - [your notes] [Second single (July 1, 2026). The video, directed by Jack Shep, has a spotlight chasing a runner. It is the third attempt at recording the song and was cut essentially live, which gives it its abrasive feel. The lyrics are about self-control. Pitchfork calls it a rousing anthem full of raw rage, and Rolling Stone UK praised how loose it is.]
8. ____ “Coyote” 3:18 - FCC: fucking x1 (final chorus) - [your notes] [Haycocks and Anson-O'Connell share the vocals. Anson-O'Connell describes it as sparse, driven by percussion, and unusually angry. Pitchfork calls it sweet and sad, a lament about tourist crowds wearing down natural wonders. It is the only track Apple Music marks Explicit.]
9. ____ “Patchwork” 3:22 - [your notes] [A quiet, late-night Bradbury song for guitar and voice. Pitchfork groups it with the raw, melancholy ballads, with stray piano notes and audible finger noise on the strings.]
10. ____ “Take My Leaving As I Love You” 2:43 - [your notes] [A stripped-down early take from pre-production in Humphrey's upstate NY house. The rest of the band insisted on keeping it over Haycocks' objections. Rolling Stone UK praised the balladry.]
11. ____ “You Could Have Died There On The Dancefloor” 2:43 - [your notes] [Closing track: an early demo with new vocals and an added guitar. The band chose a quietly uplifting ending, and NME reads its last line, about not being perfect but trying, as the album's mission statement.]
