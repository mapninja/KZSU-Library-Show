Album / Artist: Pugnello / Gilla Band

Label: Rough Trade Records

Release Date: September 25, 2026
Review Date: 10/18/2026
Reviewer: DJ Stace

General Comments / Reviews:

Abrasive, industrial-flecked noise rock and post-punk from Dublin, their first album in four years. Critics call it their most punishing yet, and too bad six of the ten tracks are FCC. - DJ Stace  [DRAFT, edit or replace]

“Dublin rock band deliver their most punishing record to date” - The Quietus

“four people going feral in a rage room” - Paste

Release Notes:

Dara Kiely - vocals; Alan Duggan Borges - guitar; Daniel Fox - bass; Adam Faulkner - drums. No guests credited.
Produced by the band; recorded and mixed by bassist Daniel Fox in Dublin (Hellfire, Yellow Door, Sonic Studios). Fox has also produced Sprints, Lambrini Girls and Mandy, Indiana, who have supported Gilla Band on tour (musicOMH). No mastering credit found.
Fourth album from the Dublin noise-rock band, formerly Girl Band (Holding Hands With Jamie, The Talkies, Most Normal). Borges runs the solo project Null Club (noise techno), Faulkner has toured on drums with Lankum, and Kiely painted the sleeve.
Built around a PĒRKONS HD-01 drum machine and synth, with noise guitar and distortion on top (Paste, The Quietus). The opener's drum kit uses five snares (Paste).
About grief over Kiely's estranged father's death in late 2022, and his schizophrenia diagnosis (Paste, DIY). The closer is named for the 1975 debut by his father's prog band, Cromwell.

Tracklist:
1. The Angelus 03:30 - FCC “tits” x1, “piss” x1 (no section labels posted). Opens on a distorted drone before the drums erupt (Treble), then a drum and bass-like frenzy of guitar noise and screamed, street-preacher vocals (Joyzine, The Quietus). Sonic Youth-style guitar noise over a kit with five snares (Paste). Tempo sources disagree (~83 or ~166 BPM).
2. Placeholder 04:14 - FCC “pissed” x1, “piss” x1 (Verse 2), “fuck” x1 (Verse 5). Slow to midtempo (~83 BPM). Fuzzy techno stomp with a big, dirty bass line and distorted synths (Joyzine, musicOMH). Half-sung, half-shouted vocal that climbs to shredded screams; the beat drops out halfway into a noise section (Treble, Joyzine). Partly about comfort distractions and childhood nostalgia (Kiely, The Line of Best Fit).
3. Alogia 03:24 - Uptempo (~134 BPM). Lurching electro groove on a catchy, looping bass line that builds toward a distorted blowup (Treble, Joyzine). Fuck Buttons-like acid ramble (Joyzine). About Kiely's schizophrenia diagnosis; the title means poverty of speech.
4. Three Hands Clapping 02:08 - Shortest track. Clicks, clacks and steam-train feedback in a whirlpool spin (Joyzine). About friends growing up and moving on (Joyzine).
5. Jacobsons 04:38 - FCC “fuck” x1 (no section labels posted). Pulsing, buzzing machine beat that intensifies with repetition, punctuated by blasts of distortion (Treble). Slower burn than most of the album, a hollowed-out dance beat with some LCD Soundsystem in it (Joyzine, musicOMH). Vocal ends in a screamed refrain. About grief over his estranged father.
6. The Megazord 05:28 - FCC “pissed” x2 (no section labels posted). Slouching, marching groove with deep bass, buzz and static (Joyzine, Treble). Screeching, stuttering synths nearly swallow the screamed vocal (Paste).
7. Computer Sadness 03:44 - FCC unverified: no lyrics posted. Shoulder-shuffle bop with layered, echoing vocals and stabbing digital bleeps (Joyzine, Post-Trash). Atonal scrapes across the stereo field (Paste). Starts as a crooned love song that dissolves into noise (The Quietus).
8. Giraffe 04:28 - FCC “shit” x5 (Chorus x2, Verse 1 x3), “fucker” x1 (Verse 1). Caution “shite” x2 (Chorus). Mid to uptempo (~127 BPM). Droning, oppressive loop with metallic guitars and a driving bass line, the closest the album comes to rock (Treble, Joyzine, Far Out). Stooges and Fall touches (Joyzine). Final minute and three-quarters switches to a house and techno pulse (Treble).
9. Crisp Pop 07:42 - FCC “pisshead” x3, “pissing” x1, “shit” x1 (no section labels posted). Longest track. Jaunty, clattering canter with the album's peppiest percussion, slinky bleeping bass and woody digital claps (Post-Trash, DIY, musicOMH). Conversational, pub-chat vocal; breaks down to a quiet stretch midway (Joyzine, The Quietus). Tempo not found.
10. At the Gallop 03:00 - Closer. Heavily vocoder-processed, robotic vocal, with blurred breakbeats and dubby bass (DIY, Paste). Saunters, with brief bursts of harsh noise; the vocal ends distorted beyond recognition (Joyzine, Post-Trash). Neil Young's Trans is cited as an inspiration (Joyzine). Some sources list it as "The Gallop".
