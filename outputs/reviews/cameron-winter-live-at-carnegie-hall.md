Album / Artist: Live at Carnegie Hall / Cameron Winter

Label: Partisan Records

Release Date: October 9, 2026
Review Date: 10/18/2026
Reviewer: DJ Stace

General Comments / Reviews:

Voice and piano only, the Geese frontman alone at Carnegie Hall with seven Heavy Metal songs, two from the Singles EP and three new ones, most stretched longer than the studio versions. Five tracks are FCC, so plan around them. - DJ Stace  [DRAFT, edit or replace]

“Live at Carnegie Hall is his star-is-born moment” - Paste

“captures Cameron Winter’s brilliance in sharper definition than ever before” - The Line of Best Fit

Release Notes:

Solo set: Cameron Winter - vocals, Steinway grand piano. First live album, recorded in the Stern Auditorium at Carnegie Hall, New York, December 2025 (Wikipedia, Paste).
Produced and mixed by Ariel Rechtshaid (mixed at Heavy Duty Studios in Burbank), mastered by Heba Kadry (Wikipedia liner notes). Rechtshaid is better known for work with Vampire Weekend, Haim and Adele (Wikipedia).
Lineage: Winter fronts the band Geese. His studio debut Heavy Metal (2024) was produced by Winter with Loren Humphrey, with James Richardson (Real Estate, MGMT's live band) on guitar, trumpet and French horn (Wikipedia).
Set list: seven songs from Heavy Metal (tracks 2, 4, 5, 6, 7, 9, 10), two from his 2024 Singles EP (tracks 11, 12) and three new, unreleased songs (tracks 1, 3, 8). Every returning song runs longer live, and the studio horn and band parts become piano (Paste).

FCCs: 2,3,6,9,10

RIYL: Geese, Tom Waits, Van Morrison, Harry Nilsson, Laura Nyro

Play: All but FCCs, Favs Rated with up to *****

Tracklist:
1. It All Fell in the River 04:30 - Midtempo (~99 BPM). New, unreleased song (not on Heavy Metal). Piano and voice, fairly uncomplicated arrangement; tempo drifts, quickening in verse 2, then he hits the keys harder (Our Culture). Set opener, starts after about 30 seconds of crowd cheering (Daily Campus). About surrendering to your emotions (Paste).
2. Try as I May 05:35 - FCC “shitless” x1 (Verse 2); caution “bitching” x1 (Verse 7). From Heavy Metal (live 5:35, studio 4:47). Piano and voice, long pauses between words and a recurring falsetto, ends on a melancholy note (Paste, Our Culture). Rubato piano: tempo listings ~116 live, ~120 studio BPM, check by ear.
3. Emperor XIII in Shades 04:18 - FCC “fucking” x1 (Verse 1.5 on the plain Genius page; the Live at Carnegie Hall page lacks it, so confirm by ear). New, unreleased song (not on Heavy Metal). Slow, brooding build with a reverent, church-like mood, sweeping melody and a big outro, then a short piano jaunt at the end (Daily Campus, Our Culture, Paste). Live listing says ~117 BPM but critics describe it as slow, so check by ear.
4. The Rolling Stones 04:25 - Caution “goddamn” x1 (Verse 3). From Heavy Metal (live 4:25, studio 3:18, album opener). The studio guitar intro becomes a piano melody in the vein of Carole King or Joni Mitchell (Paste). Midway piano solo with a rushing arpeggio and a long held trill; vocal goes from loud to tender falsetto at the end (Daily Campus, Our Culture). Tempo listings disagree (live ~95, studio ~72 BPM).
5. Love Takes Miles 04:13 - Uptempo (~136 BPM). From Heavy Metal (live 4:13, studio 3:18, studio ~130 BPM). His poppiest, most accessible song, here a more urgent, restless, breathless piano arrangement (Paste, Our Culture).
6. Drinking Age 05:10 - FCC “shit” x1 (Genius posts no section labels). From Heavy Metal (live 5:10, studio 3:30). Piano replaces the studio French horn (Paste). Sparse, staggered piano with soft-loud cycles and long silences, a downtrodden near-groan vocal, falsetto runs at the end and laughter from the crowd (Daily Campus, Paste). Live listing ~81 BPM, studio ~134 (possibly double-time).
7. Cancer of the Skull 07:03 - From Heavy Metal (live 7:03, studio 5:31). Piano riffs cycle over and over (Paste). The only song Winter introduces to the crowd, with a long held vocal note before the verse (Paste, Our Culture). Tempo listings disagree (live ~104, studio ~180 BPM).
8. If You Turn Back Now 05:37 - New, unreleased song (not on Heavy Metal). Heartbreak ballad with cascading, overwhelming piano and an achy, warbling vocal (Paste, Daily Campus). Pace: check (live listing says ~176 BPM).
9. Nina + Field of Cops 06:37 - FCC “shit” x1 (Verse 2). From Heavy Metal (live 6:37, studio 5:52). Studio version has layered vocals and swirling piano; live is solo piano with pounding keys and escalating, frenetic volume, voice faltering near the end (Our Culture, Daily Campus, Paste). Crowd favorite. Tempo listings disagree (live ~118, studio ~79 BPM).
10. $0 08:42 - FCC “fuck” x2 (Intro). From Heavy Metal (live 8:42, studio 6:43), the longest track. Dry, near-spoken intro vocal; piano rises into piercing twinkles and falls into low hums, with long-held vowels and a roughly three-minute piano stretch (Our Culture, Paste, Daily Campus). Live listing ~91 BPM, studio ~75.
11. Take It With You 06:17 - From the 2024 Singles EP, not Heavy Metal (live 6:17, studio 4:01). Slow-building, fragile ballad with falsetto and a low note near the end (Our Culture, Daily Campus). Companion to If You Turn Back Now. Tempo listings disagree (live ~107, studio ~140 BPM).
12. Vines 04:38 - From the 2024 Singles EP, not Heavy Metal (live 4:38, studio 3:06). Closer. Slow progression with falsetto moments, delicate and vulnerable (Daily Campus). Studio listing ~75 BPM; live listing ~123 is probably double-time.
